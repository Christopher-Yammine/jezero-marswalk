# Product

## Register

brand

(The landing page is the brand surface and sets the default. The app screens, planner, Identify, 3D, helmet, command and astronaut views, are product register: design serves the task.)

## Users

- **Hackathon judges (primary).** NASA Space Apps 2026, challenge "Interplanetary Survival Guide: Martian Map". Seeing Marswalk for the first time, a few minutes each, often on a projector or a laptop. They need to grasp the idea fast and be shown it is built on real NASA data.
- **Curious visitors (secondary).** No space background. Plain-language captions must carry them.
- **The fictional users the product is designed for:** a first-time astronaut on a Mars surface walk, the crew member in the habitat who commands the walk in real time, and mission control on Earth watching 4 to 24 minutes late.
- **The team.** Teammates demoing and extending it.

## Product Purpose

Marswalk takes what robots learned about Jezero crater and turns it into what a person on foot needs: a route that avoids steep ground and sand, hazards explained in plain words, a helmet view that reads the ground ahead, and a habitat that watches over the walker through a suit tag. Success: a judge understands "rover data becomes a survival guide for a human walk" within the first scroll, and knows which of three things to click.

## Brand Personality

Cinematic, grounded, protective. The tone of a film title sequence about a real mission: big, slow, confident, never jokey, never hyped. Every dramatic image is backed by a real number or a named NASA or USGS source.

## Anti-references

- Generic "space startup" pages: purple gradients, glowing neon grids, glass cards, star-field wallpaper.
- Fake or AI-generated Mars imagery. Only real NASA and USGS data and models.
- Demo modes and dummy data anywhere the public can see.
- Walls of explanatory text and screens where it is unclear what to click first.
- Research-demo caveats cluttering the interface.

## Design Principles

1. **From robot to human.** The story is the hand-off: the rover's instruments become the astronaut's senses. Layout, motion and copy should keep making that link.
2. **One obvious next step.** Every screen has a single primary action; everything else waits behind it.
3. **Real or not shown.** Real data, named sources, honest empty states. No simulation presented as live.
4. **Say it for a first-timer.** Plain words first, the technical term second.
5. **Mars carries the colour.** The planet's own rust, ochre and dusty sky do the work, not decoration.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Readable on a projector in a lit room and on a phone outdoors: strong contrast, large type for anything that matters.
- Respect "reduce motion": the scroll sequence must have a still, readable version.
- Keyboard reachable controls with visible focus; nothing important conveyed by colour alone.
- Works on a mid-range phone: a lighter 3D path where the full models are too heavy.
