Read the challenge again and did some digging on the data side. Here's what I think we build and what I'll take.

The idea in one line: a Marswalk planner. An astronaut picks a science target in Jezero, the app draws a safe route on top of real NASA data, and while they walk, a wearable tag puts them live on the same map. The challenge literally says "plan and carry out a Marswalk" so we cover both halves.

Why Jezero works for us
- It has the best data stack on Mars. NASA Trek serves a 25 cm HiRISE mosaic, a 6 m CTX mosaic, THEMIS day/night thermal, MOLA/HRSC relief, all as plain map tiles we can load straight into a browser. I tested the links today, they work.
- Perseverance's full path is public (46 km, 559 drives, with elevation at every point). I recomputed the distance and got the same number NASA publishes, so we can prove our route maths against a rover that actually drove it. https://mars.nasa.gov/mmgis-maps/M20/Layers/json/M20_traverse.json
- Weather and radiation have real numbers to cite: MEDA on Perseverance for temperature/dust, RAD on Curiosity for radiation (about 0.21 mGy per day on the surface, Hassler et al. 2014 in Science).

Safety rules the app enforces (that's the "astronaut safety first" part)
- 30 min walk-back limit, about 1.8 km at 3.5 km/h. From a 2026 NASA JSC suit paper: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20260005410/downloads/Mars_Mass_ICES2026_Presentation_Final.pdf
- Slope limit (no published Mars number, we label it draft and make it adjustable)
- Daylight-only walking window from the temperature swing
- Running radiation dose counter
- Buddy rule: two astronauts, alarm if they separate

Where your rover research plugs in
- Every obstacle a rover failed on becomes a rule or a hazard layer in the planner. That's the link between the research split and the tool.

What I'll take
- The map base: real NASA layers + Perseverance track in a browser map. Helena, that gives you a working base to make interactive.
- The route engine and safety rules above.
- The wearable tag. I already have an ESP32 tag + base from another project (fall detection, heart rate, radio link, console). I'll repurpose it as the suit tag so there's a physical demo on the table.
- Perseverance as my rover for the research split.

One honest note: Jezero is outside the subsurface ice belt (SWIM map, https://swim.psi.edu). I'd show that on the map as a finding, not hide it.
