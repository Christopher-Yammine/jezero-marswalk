Identify the ground

Pick a Perseverance photo or upload your own. A terrain model trained on rover images labelled by people colours each part of the ground as soil, bedrock, sand or big rock, and explains what that means for someone on foot. It runs entirely in this browser: nothing is uploaded.

About the model

LR-ASPP MobileNetV3 (torchvision, BSD licence), trained on 2,100 images from AI4Mars (NASA JPL, public domain), mostly Curiosity Navcam frames labelled by people. Scores on 322 test images whose labels at least three people agreed on, as overlap with the human labels (1 is perfect):

ClassOverlap

Known limits: big rocks are the weakest class; bright, washed-out sky in colour photos can be read as sand; gravel reads as plain soil. Boulder, ledge and rover-track boxes come from a second detector that runs on the habitat laptop and is not available on this page. Treat every colour as a suggestion and check with your own eyes.
