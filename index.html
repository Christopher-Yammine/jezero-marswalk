EVA budget

Walk-back: 30 min at 3.5 km/h is the 1.8 km radius proposed in NASA JSC's 2026 Mars suit mass paper. No Mars suit slope figure is published, so the slope limit is ours. Daylight window taken as 07:00 to 17:00 LMST.

Each leg is checked every 30 m; the leg's slope is its steepest step, so a straight leg across a crater is flagged even if its ends are level.

Elevation: MGS MOLA MEGDR, 128 px per degree (bins about 463 m), laser shots from 1999 to 2001, interpolated to 30 m cells. About two thirds of cells sit in bins with no laser shot (the archive fills those by interpolation): their slope is not characterized. A 463 m bin cannot show 20 m yardangs, boulders or crater rims, so a calculated slope under the limit does not mean the ground is walkable. No rover track exists here to check against. Dose uses 0.21 mGy/day, Curiosity RAD surface average at Gale (Hassler et al. 2014), not measured at Arcadia.

Elevation comes from the USGS CTX stereo DTM (20 m, MOLA-referenced) resampled to 30 m, checked against Perseverance's track elevations where both exist (see Route comparison). Three candidate routes from the habitat to the chosen target, computed on a 30 m grid of about 15 × 18 km. Shortest ignores terrain. Terrain-aware penalises ground that is dark in THEMIS night IR (loose-sand proxy) and ground with strong HRSC image texture (yardang roughness proxy). Recommended adds a preference for cells where MOLA has a laser shot and refuses steps steeper than the slope limit there. No ground has been driven or walked, so there is no proven-ground term.     Upcoming on route

    Start a walk to see route warnings.

    Position here is a simulation that walks the planned route at 60x speed. Warnings are spoken with the browser's own voice when Voice is on. Track back replans a return to the habitat from the current position on the recommended cost. The real feed is the ESP32 suit tag over LoRa (not connected on this page). Vitals stay blank until that link exists.

    Weather: when a simulated walk runs into the afternoon (after 12:00 LMST), a dust-devil alert is raised. It is simulated, not a forecast: no weather feed exists. The numbers in it describe dust devils seen in this region from orbit (afternoon formation, up to 45 m/s and 8 km tall, usually under 100 m wide; ESA/DLR release for orbit 26333).

    Arcadia: three separate questions

    • Is there buried ice? Regional evidence only. SWIM 2.0 maps (Planetary Science Institute) are about 1° cells and say ice is consistent with the data, not measured here. Ice is likely near the surface, but its depth, quantity and access are not established along any route. It is not drawn on this map as a hazard or as a water supply. orbital / regional
    • Are there visible ice-related landforms? A candidate rampart crater (about 20 km, 43.70°N 212.27°E, about 140 km north of the habitat) has a lobate ejecta apron, which suggests volatile-rich ground, possibly ice, at impact. That is an interpretation of shape, not a detection of ice. No other ice-related landform was located in the walk area. orbital
    • Can the ground be walked on? Not characterized at walk scale. The inputs are a MOLA-calculated slope at about 463 m, an HRSC texture proxy for yardangs and a THEMIS night IR proxy for loose sand. None of these measures ground strength or footing. calculated orbital proxy
